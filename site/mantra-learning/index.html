WHAT ARE WE SAYING?

A wish for a good beginning

We address Ganesha, described as having a curved trunk, a large form and the brilliance of countless suns. We ask for our work to be free from obstacles.

THINK ABOUT IT

What is a new beginning for you?

Perhaps a first day at school, a difficult drawing or a new friendship. You can pray for support and still take practical steps and ask people for help.

BREAK IT INTO LITTLE PIECES

Every word has a story.

Tap any word to hear it. The meaning below is a simple learning explanation, not a promise that difficulties will disappear.

O Ganesha, with the curved trunk and great form, shining like a crore suns: please let all my undertakings be free of obstacles, always.

FOR GROWN-UPSPROTOTYPE v0.2

Learn together, without pressure.

This is a family learning demo, not a substitute for a trained Sanskrit teacher or a verified devotional recording.

Our rule: curiosity first, accuracy always.

Ask: ‘What do these words mean to you?’ rather than ‘Why haven't you memorised it yet?’ It's fine if a child only listens today.

A simple five-minute family routine for any prayer

  1. Listen together once, without correcting every sound.
  2. Choose one line and say it slowly.
  3. Ask your child to explain one word in their own way.
  4. Relate it to a real-life challenge; encourage effort and asking for help.
  5. Stop while the child is still enjoying it.
